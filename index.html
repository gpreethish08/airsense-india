CPCB National Air Quality Spectrum CAAQMS Scale

India's National Air Quality Index (AQI) converts complex pollutant data into a single 6-tier color-coded number indicating immediate health impact:

0 - 50 Good
51 - 100 Satisfactory
101 - 200 Moderate
201 - 300 Poor
301 - 400 Very Poor
401 - 500 Severe
How CPCB Determines Overall AQI:

Sub-indices are computed for each pollutant using linear interpolation across standard breakpoints. Under CPCB rules, the overall AQI equals the maximum sub-index among all monitored pollutants ($\text{AQI} = \max(I_i)$), and that driving pollutant is designated as the Dominant Pollutant.

CPCB Sub-Index Formula: I_p = [ (I_hi - I_lo) / (B_hi - B_lo) ] × (C_p - B_lo) + I_lo

Linear interpolation across official CPCB breakpoint brackets. Minimum 3 pollutants with PM2.5 or PM10 required.

Dominant Pollutant Principle:

The overall AQI is governed by the maximum sub-index ($\max(I_1, I_2, ...)$). This ensures immediate action against acute exposure to the single most hazardous air pollutant.

Winter Spikes in North India:

Driven by post-monsoon crop residue burning, shallow boundary layer temperature inversions, and calm wind stagnation in the Indo-Gangetic Plain.
